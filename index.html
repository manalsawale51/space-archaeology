Meet the explorer

MISSION: OPPORTUNITY (MER-B)

Built to explore

NASA's Opportunity rover landed on Mars in January 2004. It was designed for a 90-Martian-day mission, but it continued exploring for almost 15 years.

A long journey

Opportunity studied Martian rocks and soil, helping scientists find evidence that liquid water had once existed on the planet's surface.

The Dust Storm

In 2018, a planet-wide dust storm darkened the Martian sky and reduced the sunlight reaching the rover.

EVIDENCE FILE 02

The Last Message

Opportunity sent its final communication to Earth on June 10, 2018. The team did not receive another message.

EVIDENCE FILE 03

The Mission's End

After months of attempts to re-establish contact, NASA declared the Opportunity mission complete in February 2019.

✓ Case file complete

Opportunity's mission ended after the 2018 global dust storm. Dust reduced sunlight reaching its solar panels, and the rover stopped communicating. NASA's team made repeated attempts to contact it before declaring the mission complete on February 13, 2019.

Why it matters: Opportunity was designed for 90 sols but operated for more than 14 years, transforming our understanding of ancient water on Mars.
